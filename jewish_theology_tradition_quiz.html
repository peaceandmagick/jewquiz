How to read this profile

Traditions represented

Orthodox Judaism

Generally affirms the binding character of halakhah and a strong continuity between rabbinic tradition and Torah. Orthodox life ranges widely, including Modern Orthodox, Haredi, Hasidic, and other communities.

Conservative / Masorti Judaism

Generally treats halakhah as binding and historically developing, with scholarly study and communal rabbinic authority playing important roles. Institutions and individual practice vary.

Reform Judaism

Often emphasizes ethical monotheism, informed individual autonomy, and an evolving Jewish tradition. Reform communities differ in their understandings of mitzvot, ritual, and peoplehood.

Reconstructionist Judaism

Often understands Judaism as an evolving religious civilization and emphasizes democratic community, Jewish culture, and a naturalistic or non-supernaturalist theology for many adherents.

Jewish Renewal

A diverse contemporary movement drawing on Jewish spiritual practice, Hasidic and mystical resources, embodied ritual, and contemporary egalitarian commitments.

Jewish Humanism / secular-cultural Judaism

Often centers Jewish peoplehood, history, culture, ethics, and communal life without requiring belief in a supernatural God or divine revelation.

Karaite Judaism

Places primary authority in the Hebrew Bible and rejects the binding authority of the rabbinic Oral Torah as understood in Rabbinic Judaism. Karaite communities themselves are diverse.

Hasidic / mystical traditionalism

A broad profile for traditions that give strong emphasis to devotional life, spiritual intention, mystical interpretations, and the inner dimensions of mitzvot. It is not a single denomination and overlaps with Orthodox halakhic life.

Designed for reflection and learning. Jewish tradition is plural; no short quiz can represent every community, historical school, or individual position.
